    Thailand October 9–15 and 24–26, 2026

    Bangkok

    I got to Thailand at the end of the rainy season, I just missed some bad flooding in Bangkok and things were mostly dry when I arrived, thankfully. Taking the metro into the city from the airport, I had a good feeling about the city, one of those things that’s kind of hard to describe. It felt lively, open and fun, I would say. And after a few days there, I think I wasn’t too far off.

    I stayed in three different neighborhoods in Bangkok, to get a little variety, and I like this picture I got of the moon coming up in the Khaosan Road area (please ignore the light streak).

    Right off the bat, one interesting fact about Thailand is that it was never colonized. The British colonized areas to the west, the French colonized areas to the east, and the Dutch and Spanish colonized areas to the south but Thailand managed to stay independent throughout those hundreds of years. There were lots of reasons for that, I recommend this book if you want to learn more.

    And well before the colonization era, the area near what’s now Bangkok, along the Chao Phraya River, established itself as a vital hub for trade throughout Asia.

    Anyway, the first morning I was in Bangkok I took a walk down to Benjakitti Park, which has a really big lake with walking/jogging/biking paths all around it and some nice trees and greenery.

    I spotted this monitor lizard swimming around. I suppose being impressed by this is like the tourists who come to New York City and are mesmerized by squirrels running around in parks; it’s something totally normal to locals but I was like, “Wow, look at that!” But you know, being easily impressed is part of the fun of traveling, haha.

    One thing I noticed pretty quickly were these little spirit houses, called san phra phum, that are all over the place. They are supposed to shelter and appease a land spirit.

    One of the big tourist attractions is the Grand Palace, where the kings and queens of Thailand have lived since 1782. The monarchy of Thailand has an interesting history, as the monarchs there still have quite a bit of power and it’s a crime to insult them. Actually the entire modern history of Thai government is interesting, they’ve had 20 constitutions so far and multiple military coups d’état, the most recent having occurred in 2014.

    And one of the big ones is Wat Pho, also known as the Temple of the Reclining Buddha. The Buddha is 46 meters (151 feet) long, and 15 meters (49 feet) tall.

    1 / 12

    Buddhism is very important in Thailand and one thing I saw happening and then learned about is that, every morning at dawn, local monks leave their monasteries and go around the locals to get food. This ritual is called tak bat, and the monks each have a bowl, and they don’t ask for anything, people already know they’re coming and have food out for them. Then, after the monks get their food, they will chant a blessing to the person. Interestingly, there is a push to give the monks healthier food.
