China April 15–27, 2026

Beijing

First stop in China: Beijing. Based in the hutong district with visits to the Forbidden City, the Temple of Heaven, the Drum and Bell Towers, the Great Wall, and more.

First off, I've been in China for a month now. So I'm going to try to catch up with some more blog posts in the near future.

When I decided to take this trip, one of my main wishes was to travel around China. I've just always been interested in the place. But I had questions: How hard would it be to get a visa? How would I get along without any Chinese language skills? Etc. These are the same things you think about going anywhere, but for some reason I thought I might have more difficulty here.

I did have to make five or six trips (I lost track) to the Chinese consulate in NYC, but to be honest that does not accurately reflect how easy it was to get permission to enter the country … half of those visits were because I messed up something, and the others were due to minor bureaucratic speedbumps. They gave me a visa and sent me on my way.

And there are a lot of online forums where people post current information about the best translation, map, and payment apps, and other general travel info, so I ended up being pretty well prepared once I got here, and most everything has worked quite well. I've tried doing a little Chinese language app, but it feels a bit silly, I'm sure I'd need a lot more time to be able to really learn anything.

As far as where to actually go, I started with the help of a few friends who've been here and who shared their thoughts and favorite destinations, so I had some good ideas about places to aim for.

In Beijing I stayed near the old center of the city, the hutong district, which surrounds the former imperial areas like the Forbidden City, Drum and Bell Towers, and other sites. Hutong date to the late 1200s, during the Mongolian Yuan dynasty. The area is mainly one to two story homes, with a few bigger buildings sprinkled in. There are hotels mixed into the residential areas though so that's where I was at. This area used to take up more space in the city but a lot was razed for new development in the 20th century. The remaining neighborhoods have some kind of protected status.

I was glad I picked this area, not just for its proximity to the main things I wanted to see, but because it had a pretty lively street life. I was there for 11 days so I feel like I got a bit of the vibe.

Forbidden City

The existing Forbidden City was constructed between 1406 and 1420. But some buildings were rebuilt or drastically repaired after fires, wars and other events. I don't usually go for group tours, but I did for this because I wanted to hear the tour guide's comments, and I'm glad I did, it was interesting.

Temple of Heaven

The Temple of Heaven was a special site where the emperor would come once a year, fast for some period of time, and then do several rituals to try and convince the gods to provide a bountiful harvest.

1 / 14

Drum and Bell Towers

These were built around the same time as the Forbidden City, and Beijing had a timekeeping system in place for hundreds of years that involved daily drumming and bell ringing. There's no drumming or ringing anymore but both towers are museums now and people use the plaza to hang out.

The Great Wall

On my last day in Beijing I took a bus out to the Jinshanling section of the Great Wall, which was about a two-and-a-half hour ride from the city. There are three separate, popular sections you can get to in a similar amount of time, but I picked that one because it has a reputation of having fewer daily visitors and more challenging terrain, and also has an unreconstructed section, which I just saw a little bit of. The Great Wall definitely lives up to the hype, it's a fantastic piece of history, plus we had great weather that day.
