South Korea August 11–14, 2026

Bonus Seoul

I missed a couple of things I really wanted to do in Seoul while I was there the first time, so I went back since it’s only two hours by high speed train from Busan (South Korea is about the size of Tennessee).

Baseball games were back on after the heat wave pause, so I went to a Doosan Bears game. The Bears are one of the original Korea Baseball Organization teams, dating to 1982, and they play at the 25,000-seat Jamsil Stadium. The Bears were playing the Hanwha Eagles, who are based in Daejeon.

I had heard about how spirited the Korean games are, and yet I was still surprised by it. Here are a couple of videos that show the crowd doing their thing. Basically the fans cheer for their team when they are up to bat, and go quieter when the other team is batting. But there are exceptions, it’s not like a strictly-enforced rule or anything that I could tell, it’s just the general custom. Each team also has their own squad of lady cheerleaders and a guy who kind of leads the chants. There are also songs and drummers and flag wavers and coordinated hand and arm movements … It’s a lot to keep track of! This first video is long, because when I was filming a Bear hit a home run, so you can see the fans going crazy. The second video is of the Eagles fans from across the stadium.

The other thing I really wanted to do was go to the War Memorial of Korea, a museum about the Korean War that started in 1950, reached a stalemate in 1953, and continues to today, as no peace agreement has ever been signed.

A lot of young people in South Korea walk around in “Korea Army,” “Korea Air Force” or “Korea Navy” clothing; t-shirts, sweatshirts, hats that have that branding, not to mention fatigues. Military service is compulsory for men and voluntary for women. It’s just a very ever-present thing.

And then of course because Seoul is rad I just walked around and saw some other stuff.
