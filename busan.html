South Korea August 4–9, 2026

Busan

Busan has a very interesting history. In modern times, after North Korean forces invaded the south in 1950 and conquered Seoul, they were able to continue pushing south. The South Korean government retreated to Busan, one of just two cities not captured by the north, and thousands of refugees fled there as well. There was great deprivation during those years, which continued even after the establishment of the border between north and south in 1953. Many of the refugees built new communities under those harsh conditions, some of which are still there and have become cultural centers.

One is called Gamcheon Culture Village, which has a lot of residences painted in bright colors and is a tourist destination. Here’s a video of it.

But of course, like most other parts of South Korea, Busan has rebounded from the war and become a bustling city. My hostel was located down near the water in the original downtown Busan neighborhood called Nampo. Here are some pictures I took around the city.

The Jagalchi fish market, reputed to be South Korea’s largest, is located in Nampo. And they had a lot more than fish, they had so many kinds of sea life for sale. A lot of people go and pick out some creature or creatures they want to eat and then they go upstairs to a restaurant where they are served a meal of the fish or whatever they just chose. I didn’t do that, but I did eat nearby at a really good seafood restaurant.

1 / 3

Busan is located on the southeastern coast of the country and is home to several really nice beaches.

The first beach I went to is called Dadaepo, and one of the first things I noticed was that the sand at the shoreline appeared to have all these gold flecks. I couldn’t find anything online about it, so maybe I was just imagining things, but it looked very gold to me. Anyway, the water was really nice to swim in, and then I hiked around a little peninsula. After all that I got some seafood and they had this crazy water/light show at 8 p.m. so I watched that.

The second beach I went to is called Haeundae Beach and it’s the big nice one with lots of fancy hotels around it and really beautiful water. And the third beach I went to is called Songjeong which supposedly a lot of Koreans prefer over Haeundae cause it’s less crowded. Although it was definitely not as picturesque or impressive. I was really looking forward to going to a baseball game in Busan, but when I was there, there was a big heat wave and the Korea Baseball Organization canceled every game in the league for a week because of it. So I would have to wait until I returned to Seoul to do that.

But I did go to a couple of history museums and the Busan Museum of Movies, which focuses on Korean cinema. I had a really nice time in Busan, it’s got a pretty relaxed vibe.
