Chongqing

Chongqing is a huge municipality in Sichuan province centered around what’s often marketed as an “8-D” city, so named due to it being built into steep, green mountains that rise up from the Yangtze and Jialing rivers, which converge there. The 8-D thing is just a tourism marketing term, of course, but it is a unique city with interesting features, like tall buildings that you can enter at the ground level, take an elevator up 10 or 20 stories, and exit at another ground level because the hill it’s on is so steep.

There are also lots of overlapping highways, metro trains that run through apartment buildings, and comically long, steep and winding public stairways. After you’ve been there a minute, you do start to interpret the space in an unusual way, looking up and down and all around to get your bearings rather than just left or right or whatever.

I was in Chongqing for about a week and I loved it, it was definitely one of my favorite places I visited. It’s a really fun city to just wander around in, there’s some really interesting modern history there, good museums, great parks, delicious spicy food … it’s fantastic. And for a big city it feels lush, with lots of parks of different sizes, green plants everywhere, the green hills, and stuff like trees growing out of walls.

Chongqing served as the capital of the Nationalist government as they and the Communists battled Japan from 1937 until the end of WWII. I went to a couple of museums that covered China’s fight against Japan and its participation in World War II (they call it the Global War Against Fascism) on the side of the allies. Of course the Communists beat the Nationalists in the civil war after WWII and the Nationalists fled to Taiwan, sparking the tension that continues to today.

I also went to a museum about the Three Gorges Dam, which was a monumental infrastructure project that displaced at least 1.3 million people and drowned many historical sites, not to mention all the flora and fauna. The museum was kind of weird, though, there wasn’t that much about the project other than videos and exhibits extolling its positive impacts. Across from the museum was the Chongqing People’s Auditorium, where government officials meet.
