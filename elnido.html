    Malapascua

    Then, the real highlight of my trip to the Philippines was that I learned how to scuba dive at this island called Malapascua. Scuba diving wasn’t really something I had planned on for this trip, but it had been recommended to me as a fun thing to do, so it was kind of in the back of my mind. I just was researching what to do in the Philippines and it turns out it’s a wonderful place to do it. So I signed up for five days of lessons and it was such a great experience. The coral reefs there are teeming with all kinds of sea life; fish, plants, crustaceans, and weird, beautiful, colorful things like nudibranchs. Unfortunately the reefs there are subject to the same climate change pressures as other coral reefs in the world, but there are still some thriving ones.

    Most of the dives I did were about 5 to 15 meters deep (about 16 to 50 feet), because that’s where most of the coral ecosystem is. You need a special camera like a GoPro or something to get pix underwater, so I wasn’t able to get any. But trust me, it was incredible.

    The last day we went to a thresher shark habitat, which is a shoal way offshore of Malapascua. We left before the sun got up, because the thresher sharks come up to the shoal in the mornings to let cleaner fish clean their skin. You aren’t guaranteed to see sharks, but we did two dives that day and saw them both times.

    It was a little freaky at first because the sharks are like, 10 feet long, and they are curious and get pretty close to you, like within 10-15 feet, but then you realize they aren’t really going to go after you and it’s cool. The other awesome thing we did was dive down the wall of the shoal to about 28 meters, which is like 92 feet. So you’re swimming around the top of the shoal and it’s a coral habitat like normal, then you see an edge … and you swim over the edge … and it just goes down into total blackness, lol. It was a strange feeling! Then you descend down the wall, and there are still coral and other marine life living and doing their normal things there, too.
