Hong Kong

You can see Hong Kong from the top of the Ping An Finance Centre in Shenzhen, and after being in China for two and a half months, it seemed strange that such a place was so close. Chinese, but operating, more or less, under the capitalist system that had been in place in Hong Kong since the British took it over in 1841.

The Brits handed Hong Kong back to the PRC in 1997, after securing a 50-year agreement that ensured things wouldn’t change very much from the status quo. Hong Kong became a Special Administrative Region, the example of the “one country, two systems” philosophy.

But Hong Kong is different in other ways, too. Cantonese is widely spoken, and Cantonese people, while considered ethnically Han, are still vulnerable to the kinds of sinicization efforts that have been carried out in Xinjiang (where the Uyghurs live) and Tibet.

I was never there before the handover, so I don’t know what the difference is, but Hong Kong definitely feels different, culturally, from anywhere I was at on the mainland.

Anyway, Hong Kong has until 2047 to enjoy whatever autonomy it retains from the handover.

The city itself reminded me a lot of New York, because of the density, the diversity, the pace and the proximity to water.
