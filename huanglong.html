China May 10–13, 2026

Huanglong & Jiuzhaigou

After being in a few big cities, I wanted to see some nature so I spent a little time in the mountains of the Ngawa Tibetan and Qiang Autonomous Prefecture of Sichuan Province, also called Aba. It’s not in Tibet, but Tibetans and Qiang people have lived there for a very long time. So there is Tibetan culture, food and writing all over, and there are Tibetan Buddhist temples in both parks I went to.

Huanglong

The first park I went to is called Huanglong, which is famous for its travertine pools and unique wildlife. Travertine is a type of limestone, and in this park, all these pools have formed over the years and when they’re filled with snow runoff, they have really amazing blue-green colors. There’s a 600-year-old Tibetan Buddhist temple at the top of the park where the pools are, which kind of got me curious about how religion is treated here.

A few years ago I got to go to Croatia and they have a park with similar geologic features there, and there are some others, but they’re pretty rare. Huanglong is at a higher elevation, and that plus the earlier time of year meant I actually had to use a jacket that day. I think I’ve only had to bust it out twice.

I was a little early in the season, so some of the pools farther down the mountain were still dry. But the ones up top were full.

Jiuzhaigou

Right after Huanglong I did two days at this big national park called Jiuzhaigou.

At both Huanglong and Jiuzhaigou, the tourist infrastructure is really developed. You get a ticket in advance, show up, get transported to a starting point (cable car at Huanglong, bus at Jiuzhaigou), follow a well-surfaced path around the site with lots of sometimes quite large rest areas with food and bathrooms and stuff, and then get transported to the exit.

And it makes sense, so many people visit these places, it would be crazy if everyone could just go wherever they wanted. These pools and other things wouldn’t last a week in their natural condition. Maybe these places are more like Yellowstone or something in the states? I’ve never been to Yellowstone, though.

Anyway, the way Jiuzhaigou is set up is there’s a town that everybody stays in, and then, during peak season from April to November, the park lets in up to 41,000 people per day. Once you’re in the park you get a bus that takes you up to a fork in the road and you pick one, take a bus to the top, and then can do a combination of walking/riding buses back down. Each branch of the fork is about 18 km (11 miles) long. There are all kinds of rad things to see.

Day 2

The second day I saw a couple of things I didn’t see the day before, but mostly took it slower and hung out longer in some places I liked. Of note is the image of the Goddess Woluosemo, or Woluo Semo — I’ve seen it both ways — that’s on a hillside. The Tibetan legend is that she is the goddess of the area, and that her lover gave her a magical mirror made of wind that a demon forced her to drop, and the broken pieces of the mirror are the more than 100 lakes in the park.
