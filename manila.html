    Philippines September 13–21, 2026

    Manila & Boracay

    Manila

    There are something like 7,600 islands in the archipelago that is the Republic of the Philippines, but I’ll only be hitting 6-8 of them. I started out in the capital city of Manila, which is on the largest and most populous island of Luzon. It was a little weird — and nice — being in a place where English is so widely spoken. Like I said before, my translation apps have been great and have enabled lots of interactions I would not otherwise have been able to enjoy, but it just wasn’t the same as being able to casually shoot the breeze with people.

    Manila has street life that I really hadn’t seen in Japan, China, Korea or Taiwan and it was a rush to start visiting there. Especially in China, the streets can get pretty crowded with cars and scooters and people and there is a lot going on … but Manila seemed to have even more. I stayed in the San Andreas district, which is one of the most densely populated parts of the metro Manila area, so I think I got a good taste of the city.

    There are lots of vehicles like jeepneys running on old diesel engines so the air quality can get pretty gnarly at street level (see this video for an example).

    Spain colonized the Philippines from about 1565 to 1898, when it signed the Treaty of Paris, ending the Spanish-American war and ceding the Philippines to the U.S.A. Filipinos fought against their exploitation over the centuries through battles, protests and diplomacy, and in the mid-1890s they started a revolution, but the Spanish didn’t leave until after the Treaty of Paris was signed. Then the Japanese defeated the Americans in 1941 and occupied the country until 1945. The Filipinos finally got their independence in 1946.

    In the book I read, the Spaniards never wanted the native population to learn Spanish, but the Americans were happy to make English the next main language after Tagalog. There are scores of other languages still spoken on the different islands.

    Here are a few photos from Intramuros, the walled section of Manila built by the Spanish. There are also some shots of a dungeon at Ft. Santiago, which is inside the walls. The underground space was originally used by the Spanish to store ammunition but it was too damp so they built a new place and used it as a prison. Then the Japanese also used it that way during World War II.

    Boracay

    My next destination was a change in gears, to say the least. I went to Boracay island, which is known for its really nice, long white sand beaches. I happened to get great weather and I went swimming in the ocean every morning. It was a very relaxing type of a place, so it was cool to spend a few days there.
