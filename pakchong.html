    Thailand October 21–24 and October 26–November 4, 2026

    Pak Chong & Chanthaburi

    I heard about this national park where wild Asian elephants roam, so I decided I would go and hopefully see some. The park is called Khao Yai National Park and it’s near a town called Pak Chong that’s northeast of Bangkok. There are a bunch of cool species that live there, from elephants to different kinds of gibbons to gaur to leopard cats.

    I got pretty excited about seeing some wild elephants roaming in the forested hills there, but, alas, it was not to be. The park is 2,166 square km (836 square miles), and although there are supposedly 140–200 elephants there, the roads and observation points are pretty minimal so you have to be kind of lucky. There are some spots with good odds, like a big salt lick and a large pond they frequent, but I went to both the day I was there and saw no elephants, just the big trails they made.

    I did see a solitary deer that was frolicking by itself at the salt lick, though. It was pretty funny, it was just getting its salt or whatever and running around and kicking its heels. I dunno, maybe there’s something else at the salt lick besides salt that the animals like?

    And I did see this hornbill flying … I was lucky to see a couple, people don’t always see them at the park, either.

    Anyway, it was OK that I didn’t see the elephants, I realized that it really doesn’t happen every day there. You can pay for a tour where the guides are in communication with park staff so if there are elephants in the area, you have a better chance, although there are still no guarantees. But I wanted to take a scooter so I could explore the park at my own pace, so you know, trade-offs.

    I did see some cool stuff, though, like this wildly expressive pileated gibbon.

    I also got bitten by a leech at Khao Yai, that was a first! I thought they hung out in the water, but no, in this forest they cling to leaves and branches, waiting for something with blood to pass by and then they latch on.

    Now, having been disappointed by the elephants, I was determined to catch some other wildlife things, and I heard there was a place you could go and watch at least one million wrinkle-lipped free-tailed bats fly out of their mountainside cave at dusk. So I took my scooter and made the trip.

    The ride itself was interesting, you go through all these back roads in the rural area outside the city of Pak Chong, which isn’t huge to begin with. Getting to the cave was really scenic and cool, and coming back in the dark was a whole other adventure, haha.

    And the bats came through like heroes. Check them out. It took like 15–20 minutes for all the bats to fly out, that’s how many there are. It was really neat to watch it. And the night I was there, there was a beautiful sunset and an almost full moon was out, so it was extra cool. They’re kind of far away so you can’t hear much but the soft whoosh of millions of wings flapping, and the occasional chirp.

    There are other things around Pak Chong that are interesting, too, so I checked out some of those.

    This mountainside Buddha at Wat Thep Phithak Punnaram was pretty cool. There was a big thunderstorm while I was there, so all the roosters and peacocks that live there congregated with us people under the sheltered area.

    Then I went to a town called Chanthaburi, which is near the southeast coast. It’s a little river town, so it was nice to walk along the river in the mornings and evenings. There were quite a few wats and some shrines that weren’t the traditional Thai Buddhist ones, they are more Chinese influenced and apparently are a blend of Chinese folk religions, Buddhism, Taoism and animism.
