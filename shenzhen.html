China June 16–July 4, 2026

Shenzhen & Guangzhou

Time flies! My visa for mainland China was due to expire on July 14, so I had to start heading to the exit (Hong Kong, where US citizens don’t need a visa to enter), and I chose to leave through Guangdong Province.

Shenzhen

Like Xiamen and Shantou, Shenzhen was one of the first four cities that were allowed to start special economic zones in 1980, but it seems to have left those two in the dust. The area they declared an SEZ was basically a small, old fishing village across the Sham Chun River, or Shenzhen River, from Hong Kong.

So after all that investment and attention from the Chinese Communist Party, it has grown into one of the country’s four main Tier 1 cities and is a large technology manufacturing center. Because it’s so new, it feels very different from every other Chinese city I visited, which usually have long, long histories. Certainly Guangzhou, to Shenzhen’s north, and Hong Kong, to its south, have those long histories, but Shenzhen is just kind of all newish — and still growing and developing.

Here are some pictures from around town, including from the top of the Ping An Finance Centre, which, according to the Guinness Book of World Records, has the “highest glass floor cantilevered observation deck” at just about 548 meters, or 1,797 feet.

Guangzhou

Guangzhou, aka Canton, is the capital of Guangdong Province. Its history dates back thousands of years, and for hundreds of years it’s been an area where many different cultures have mixed to trade. The Cantonese culture and language is still strong there.

I was just there for a few days but it was a really neat place. One cool site I went to was the Shawan ancient town, an 800-year old town that’s on the way south to Shenzhen.

1 / 5

And they have a few really cool homes with oyster shell walls, which apparently helped homes in the area stay cool and dry and repelled insects.

1 / 2

I also went to Baomo Garden and Nanyue Yuan, two gardens near Shawan ancient town. Baomo Garden was an actual old garden that was rebuilt after destruction, while Nanyue Yuan was constructed recently.

Baomo Garden had a really cool little stream full of koi that you could take boat rides on, which I did. You can see a koi feeding frenzy in one of the videos.
