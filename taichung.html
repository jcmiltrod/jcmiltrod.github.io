    Taiwan August 28–September 3, 2026

    Taichung, Tainan & Kenting

    Taichung

    After Taipei I went to my friends’ town of Taichung, which is the Taiwanese city with the most green space, like parks, I learned. It’s a beautiful place. The weather was quite hot and sometimes rainy — it was the rainy season in Taiwan when I was there — but we still got out and did some fun stuff.

    The hungry ghost festival was still happening so we saw a shrine inside a day market that was having a ceremony.

    And one photo of the altar:

    One afternoon my friend took me shrimp fishing, which is where you go to this big building that has large pools stocked with big Thai shrimp. You sit by the side and fish with a pole and then after your time is up (you pay for an hour or two or three, etc.), you go over to a grill area and grill the shrimp you caught. The place has its own restaurant, so you can order a bunch of other kinds of food, too, to go with your shrimp.

    Tainan

    From Taichung I separated from my friends and took a train to Tainan, the oldest city in Taiwan and its capital until the late 1800s, when it was switched to Taipei. I stayed in an older section of town that’s known for historic buildings and such. I took a bunch of pictures walking around one evening there, here are some of those.

    Tainan is also famous for its food offerings so I tried a bunch of delicious stuff, including more bitter tea, ha ha. The aboriginal cultures are represented in one part of town, so I went there to see the cultural center, but unfortunately not much was going on when I went there.

    Kenting

    Then it was on to Kenting, or Kending, I saw it spelled both ways. That’s a little beach town all the way at the southern end of the island. Supposedly it’s known for its nice weather, but there was a big storm both days I was there, lol, so I had to go check it out between downpours. Still a cool place, though.
