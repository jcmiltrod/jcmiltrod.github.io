Xi’an & Chengdu

Xi’an

After Shanghai, I spent a couple of days in Xi’an, the city that’s near the mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang, who is credited as being the first leader to unify the various entities that would be considered China. Before his death in 210 BCE, he ordered the construction of a necropolis for himself. It contains the famous terracotta warriors and horses, but only a fraction of those have been unearthed and there are also musicians and acrobats and apparently it’s like a whole underground city or palace (good article about it here). They haven’t excavated other parts of it because they’re afraid they’ll damage things, and are waiting for technology to improve.

It is a really interesting sight to see. For the biggest pit, they constructed an airplane hangar-sized building, reconstructed a lot of figures and then placed them in rows.

I was there during a “Golden Week” holiday, when much of the country gets a week off work, so the site was probably as busy as it gets. All the travel advice is, “Never go anywhere famous during a Golden Week,” because the number of domestic tourists skyrockets and sites get packed, but it was interesting to appreciate the stuff with everyone else. These videos sort of capture the crowd size, noise and chaos, but not really. All my nights in pits of the punk rock variety paid off in terms of being squished and jostled and pushed around still having a good time.

1 / 3

There are four Golden Week holidays throughout the year, and this one is always around International Worker’s Day on May 1. But I learned from locals that the workers who got May 1 off (it fell on a Friday this year) had to work a Saturday afterwards to make up for it, which … doesn’t make any sense to me, lol.

Xi’an itself is a neat town, and I stayed in the Muslim Quarter, which is a really old and crowded and busy part of the city. I had a great time just walking around there, looking at things and eating the street food. It’s within the old city walls, which are still intact and upon which you can walk or rent a bicycle. I mostly rode a bike but also walked a bit of it. Ever since I got here, I found that some Chinese people want to take pictures with me. I don’t know exactly why they want the pics, but it’s usually a fun and charming interaction, even if we can’t speak each other’s languages. Chengdu

After Xi’an, which is in Shaanxi province, I went to Chengdu, which is in Sichuan province. Chengdu has the reputation of being kind of a cool city, with a music scene and stuff. I had a sinus infection, though, so I didn’t get out as much as I wanted.

I still saw a bunch of stuff, including People’s Park, which is a really beautiful space with lots of trees and plants and flowers and little lakes and ponds and streams. It’s also home to the Railway Protection Movement museum and a big monument to the people who died in that struggle.

One interesting thing at the park are these notices of availability for marriage. There is one section for men and one for women (blue and pink, obviously). Each paper has details about the person. And often their older relatives circulate among the notices to connect with the relatives of potential matches.

And Pandas! I went to the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding early in the morning because that’s when pandas are most active, I heard. And I was not disappointed, I saw lots of pandas. The park was huge and I didn’t see it all, but I saw enough pandas for one day, if such a thing can be true. And not just the black and white ones, the red ones, too. And peacocks.

And I did a day trip from Chengdu to Leshan, which is home to the world’s largest stone buddha. It’s located at the confluence of the Min, Qingyi and Dadu rivers. Apparently the confluence was very dangerous for ships, so the Buddha was intended to help sailors get through safely. And in fact, the large amount of rock that went into the river during the carving of the Buddha changed the riverbed enough that the water was somewhat becalmed. Some sources say the Buddha was an artful way to execute the river control project.
